\begin{abstract}
Reasoning traces are usually curated for their content. We show that their
\emph{language} --- whether the same derivation is written as prose or as a
formal proof --- is itself a causal lever on what a model learns. We construct
paired training corpora in which every prompt, answer, and latent derivation
is identical and only the trace rendering differs, and we fine-tune matched
models on each side of the pair. The choice of trace language then determines
how reasoning generalizes beyond the training distribution. When training
covers only shallow derivations, natural-language traces generalize better.
Once training covers sufficient depth, the comparison reverses sharply: formal
supervision lifts out-of-distribution answer accuracy from $17.8\pm4.7$ to
$75.0\pm9.5$ percent at \passk{1}, with $98.1\pm0.5$ percent of test prompts
having a correct \emph{and} machine-valid proof within sixteen samples.
Because formal traces are checkable, this coverage is harvestable without any
answer key: an answer-blind first-valid selector converts sixteen samples into
$97.3\pm1.1$ percent single-answer accuracy, whereas the same selector
recovers only $33.8\pm1.4$ percent from natural-language traces. We
characterize when the lever matters --- depth coverage, model capacity, and
shortcut structure all modulate it --- and complete a preregistered transfer
study that midtrains Qwen2.5-7B on a production mixture with five percent
matched formal or natural-language derivations. Replacing five percent of the
mixture is benchmark-neutral, and both trace conditions roughly double
instructed answer-format compliance on long-context multi-hop tasks, but an
example-level decomposition shows no reasoning gain transfers at this scale
--- a null we trace to the training pipeline splitting nearly half of all
derivations mid-proof, destroying exactly the depth structure the controlled
study identifies as necessary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Chain-of-thought supervision trains language models to produce intermediate
steps before answering~\citep{wei2022chain,kojima2022large}. Almost all such
supervision is written in natural language. This convention is so uniform that
it hides an experimental variable: the same derivation can be rendered as
discursive prose or as a sequence of explicit rule applications, and the two
renderings present very different token-level learning signals. This paper
asks what that choice does to the reasoning a model learns.

The question cannot be answered with ordinary corpora, because two solutions
written in different styles rarely contain the same computation. We therefore
build paired synthetic corpora. A deterministic generator first samples a
latent derivation, then renders it either as a compact formal proof or as a
controlled natural-language explanation. The prompt, answer, facts, rules, and
proof graph are identical within each pair; only the trace language changes
(Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). Any difference between models trained on the two
sides of the pair is attributable to representation, not to problem selection
or answer quality. We study training, not tool use: no prover is called at
inference time, and both models generate under matched token budgets.

Three findings emerge. First, \emph{the trace language changes how reasoning
generalizes, and the direction of the effect depends on depth coverage}. With
shallow-to-intermediate training ranges, natural-language supervision is
reliably stronger out of distribution. Once the training range reaches depth
25, the comparison reverses by a wide margin: formal supervision reaches
$75.0\pm9.5$ percent out-of-distribution answer \passk{1} on our branching
deduction task versus $17.8\pm4.7$ for the matched natural condition
(Figure~\ref{fig:crossover}). Length and token-budget controls show the gap is
not explained by target length or by the number of supervised tokens. An
independently generated constraint-propagation task shows the same
correctness advantage.

Second, \emph{formal traces pay a verifiability dividend}. Nearly every test
prompt has a correct and mechanically valid formal proof somewhere in sixteen
samples (joint \passk{16} $98.1\pm0.5$ percent), and because validity is
checkable without the answer key, that coverage is deployable: selecting the
first sample accepted by the proof checker yields $97.3\pm1.1$ percent
single-answer accuracy. The identical selector applied to translated
natural-language traces recovers only $33.8\pm1.4$ percent, in large part
because long natural traces frequently truncate before completing a checkable
derivation. Verifiable representations thus convert sampling headroom into
top-1 accuracy with no reward model and no gold labels --- the test-time
analogue of reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards.

Third, \emph{the lever has boundary conditions, and they carry over to
practice}. Model capacity reorders the comparison: at 32B parameters the
single-modality advantage flips toward natural language at \passk{1} even
though formal coverage at \passk{16} remains near ceiling, and a
mode-conditioned 32B model improves its formal mode by 21.1 points over
formal-only training. Concatenating both traces in one target collapses
generalization; spurious-marker training removes the formal advantage. We then
test the practical claim end to end with a preregistered three-way transfer
study: midtraining Qwen2.5-7B~\citep{qwen2025qwen25} for five billion tokens
on the Dolmino mixture~\citep{olmo2025olmo2} with five percent of tokens
replaced by matched formal or natural-language derivations, followed by
identical instruction tuning. The substitution is benchmark-neutral on a
ten-task suite, and both trace conditions roughly double compliance with an
instructed answer format on long-context multi-hop tasks, but an example-level
decomposition attributes the entire apparent multi-hop gain to that compliance
effect: conditioned on producing an extractable answer, the three models are
statistically indistinguishable. We diagnose why: the packing pipeline split
44--48 percent of derivation documents across training windows, destroying
precisely the deep intact structure that the controlled study identifies as
necessary for the formal advantage.

Our contributions are:
(i) a paired-trace experimental design, with an audited unique-answer
generator and matched evaluation budgets, that isolates trace representation
as a causal training variable;
(ii) the finding that representation changes out-of-distribution multi-hop
generalization, with a sharp depth-coverage crossover, replicated across two
task families and characterized across capacity, shortcut, and hybrid
regimes;
(iii) a demonstration that machine-checkability is itself a training-relevant
property: an answer-blind verifier turns formal sampling coverage into
near-ceiling top-1 accuracy;
(iv) a preregistered, fully matched midtraining transfer study, with an
honest decomposition separating reasoning transfer from response-format
transfer, and a mechanism-level account of why the synthetic advantage does
not yet transfer at this scale.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Reasoning traces.}
Chain-of-thought prompting and supervised rationales improve multi-step
reasoning in sufficiently large language models~\citep{wei2022chain,
kojima2022large}. Subsequent work constructs new rationales through
bootstrapping~\citep{zelikman2022star}, decomposes hard questions into smaller
ones~\citep{zhou2023least}, and searches over candidate reasoning
states~\citep{yao2023tree}. Scratchpad supervision shows that an
autoregressive model can use generated intermediate state to solve algorithmic
tasks~\citep{nye2021show}. These studies establish the value of intermediate
computation; we study the representation in which that computation is taught.
Controlled studies of synthetic reasoning data, notably the iGSM family,
probe what pretraining on structured problems teaches a
model~\citep{ye2024physics}; we add the representation axis to that program by
holding the problems fixed and varying only the trace language.

\paragraph{Programs and symbolic representations.}
Program-aided language models and program-of-thought prompting use executable
code for intermediate computation~\citep{gao2023pal,chen2023program}.
Logic-based methods translate problems into symbolic forms and use an external
solver or verifier~\citep{pan2023logiclm,ling2023deductive}. Formal theorem
proving trains models to construct machine-checkable proofs in systems such as
Lean~\citep{polu2020generative,jiang2022draft,leandojo2023,leanCopilot2024}.
Our setting differs in two respects: the same model performs all generation,
and every formal target has a paired natural-language target derived from the
same proof, so the training effect of representation can be measured without
changing the inference mechanism.

\paragraph{Faithfulness and verification.}
Natural-language rationales can omit factors that influence a prediction or
rationalize an answer after the fact~\citep{turpin2023language,
lanham2023measuring}. Formal syntax makes local correctness mechanically
testable, but it does not make a generated trace valid by construction. We
report answer accuracy and proof validity separately throughout, and we show
that checkability has direct practical value for test-time selection, which
connects to verifiable-reward posttraining~\citep{yue2025does}.

\paragraph{Controlled generalization.}
Synthetic tasks such as SCAN and COGS expose failures of systematic
generalization by controlling train and test
distributions~\citep{lake2018generalization,keysers2020measuring}. We adopt
the same use of synthetic data as a measurement instrument: our main shift
increases proof depth while preserving the rule system, and a
spurious-marker shift tests whether representation changes shortcut
use~\citep{geirhos2020shortcut}.

\section{A Paired-Trace Testbed}
\label{sec:paired}

\subsection{Tasks and trace construction}

Each example begins with a latent directed acyclic proof. The generator emits
a prompt containing ground facts, rules, a query, and distractor branches,
then traverses the supporting path to produce the target answer. A rendering
function writes that path in one of two trace languages. The \formal{}
rendering declares constants and predicates and applies named inference rules
to numbered lines. The \nat{} rendering expresses the same substitutions and
conclusions, line for line, in controlled sentences. Both targets end with the
same answer field, and the prompt is natural language in both conditions.

\branchproof{} tests composition across a chain of Horn-style inferences in
which every step offers four locally plausible branches, exactly one of which
is derivable from the current state. Every layer introduces a fresh constant,
and the generator computes the full Horn closure of each example, accepting it
only if exactly one candidate answer is derivable. \attrcon{} propagates
values among object attributes under generated constraints; its vocabulary and
proof construction are disjoint from \branchproof{}, providing an independent
test of the hypothesis. Appendix~\ref{sec:paired-example} gives complete
paired targets; Appendices~\ref{sec:grammar} and~\ref{sec:generator-eval}
specify the grammar, generator, and validators, and
Appendix~\ref{sec:uniqueness-audit} documents the answer-uniqueness audit of
the final generator.

\subsection{Models, training, and evaluation}

The main experiment fine-tunes OLMo-3-7B with LoRA on 50{,}000 materialized
examples per condition, with maximum training depth in
$\{5,10,15,20,25\}$ and three seeds per condition. We evaluate at depths
$\{1,2,5,10,12,15,18,20,25,30,35,40,45,50\}$ with 32 prompts per depth, 16
samples per prompt at temperature 1.0 plus greedy decoding. Both trace
languages receive an identical 7{,}168-token generation limit inside a
16{,}384-token window; a tokenizer audit confirms every gold prompt and
target fits. Depths above the maximum training depth are out of distribution
(OOD); for train-through-25 this means depths 30--50. Joint metrics require
the correct answer \emph{and} a mechanically valid proof: formal traces are
checked directly by the proof engine, and natural traces are checked by a
deterministic translation into the same engine, so we also report answer-only
accuracy, which involves no translation step. Training and evaluation details
appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:training-details}; metric definitions in
Appendix~\ref{sec:metrics}.

\paragraph{The crossover.}
Figure~\ref{fig:crossover} shows the central phenomenon. For maximum training
depths 5 through 20, natural-language supervision is the better extrapolator:
it holds 50--68 percent OOD greedy accuracy while formal models stay below 35
percent. Training through depth 25 reverses the ranking decisively. Formal
models jump to $82.1\pm7.0$ percent OOD greedy accuracy while natural models
fall to $48.7\pm21.7$, and the sampled \passk{1} gap widens to $57.1\pm14.0$
points in favor of formal supervision --- positive in every seed. The trace
language is not a stylistic choice: it determines \emph{which} generalization
regime the model enters, and its benefit switches sign as depth coverage
grows. A natural interpretation is that the model must see enough deep
composition before the regularity of the formal language becomes exploitable;
Section~\ref{sec:transfer} shows this coverage condition has direct practical
consequences.

\paragraph{Magnitude at full coverage.}
Table~\ref{tab:main} details the train-through-25 endpoint. Formal supervision
more than quadruples OOD answer \passk{1} (75.0 vs.\ 17.8) and the gain is not
purchased by sacrificing validity: joint accuracy improves nearly as much
(68.5 vs.\ 17.5), and at the full sampling budget $98.1\pm0.5$ percent of OOD
prompts admit a correct and valid proof. At depth 50 --- twice the training
horizon --- formal models retain 57.2 percent answer \passk{1} and 99.0
percent answer \passk{16}, versus 1.9 and 21.9 percent for natural traces. The
independently constructed \attrcon{} task shows the same correctness
advantage (89.6 vs.\ 80.8 at \passk{16}), while its joint comparison favors
the natural condition, so we treat \attrcon{} as replicating the correctness
effect rather than every aspect of the \branchproof{} result.

\paragraph{Why natural traces fail deep.}
Raw generations locate the failure. At depths 30--50, natural-language models
frequently re-copy the long premise list and run into the generation limit
without ever closing: 27--32 percent of retained natural samples lack a final
answer, versus 1.1--2.8 percent of formal samples under the identical budget.
The compact formal syntax keeps deep derivations inside the budget and keeps
the model on the derivation rather than on discourse.

\paragraph{Surface controls.}
The gap is not explained by target length or token count. Padding formal
atoms with distractor symbols (matching natural-language target length while
preserving the grammar) retains $67.3\pm17.5$ percent OOD \passk{1}, and a
natural condition trained under a matched target-token budget reaches only
$41.0\pm20.1$. Neither target length nor the number of supervised tokens
accounts for the main gap; the structure of the trace itself carries the
effect. Throughout the paper we report single-sample and sampled-coverage
metrics together, because greedy and \passk{1} readouts systematically
understate the formal condition: its sampled distribution retains
near-ceiling coverage even in regimes where its top-1 accuracy loses
(Sections~\ref{sec:verifier} and~\ref{sec:boundary}).

High \passk{16} is often dismissed as an oracle metric: it certifies that a
correct solution exists in the distribution, not that a system can find it. A
formal trace language removes that objection, because validity is decidable
without the answer. We retain all sixteen samples for every train-through-25
prompt and select the first sample whose proof the checker accepts.

Table~\ref{tab:verifier-selection} shows the result. Answer-blind selection
lifts formal OOD answer accuracy from $75.4\pm11.0$ to $97.3\pm1.1$ percent
--- recovering nearly all of the $99.8$ percent oracle ceiling --- and joint
accuracy to $96.7\pm1.1$. Seed variance collapses by an order of magnitude:
verification converts a good-but-noisy sampler into a reliable system. The
identical selector applied to natural traces (via translation into the same
checker) reaches only $33.8\pm1.4$ percent, bounded both by lower coverage
and by the truncated, uncheckable traces described above. A max-line-validity
variant performs comparably (96.5/96.0). Selection here uses validity only;
oracle numbers are reported solely as coverage ceilings.

This is the test-time counterpart of reinforcement learning from verifiable
rewards: the same checkability that would supply a reward signal already
converts sampling headroom into top-1 accuracy with no training step at all.
Whether posttraining can internalize the selector's gains into \passk{1}
directly is a natural follow-up that our released checkpoints support; we
deliberately do not infer it from the selection result.

\section{Boundary Conditions: When Does the Lever Matter?}
\label{sec:boundary}

The crossover in Figure~\ref{fig:crossover} already shows the formal advantage
is conditional on depth coverage. Four further experiments map the boundary.

\paragraph{Capacity reorders the comparison.}
On Qwen2.5-7B the two conditions are statistically close at \passk{1}
($69.9\pm11.1$ formal vs.\ $74.9\pm7.0$ natural). At OLMo-3-32B the
single-modality comparison flips hard toward natural language ($98.9\pm0.8$
vs.\ $63.9\pm5.1$ answer \passk{1}): a stronger base model extrapolates prose
reliably. Formal coverage remains near ceiling ($98.8\pm0.9$ answer
\passk{16}), so the 32B formal model still \emph{contains} correct proofs but
assigns them less probability mass under unconstrained sampling --- precisely
the regime where the verifier dividend of Section~\ref{sec:verifier} applies.

\paragraph{Both languages can live in one model.}
Concatenating the two renderings inside a single target fails completely in
both orders ($0.5\pm0.4$ and $0.2\pm0.1$ percent OOD \passk{1}): the model
copies one or both long traces and exhausts the budget. Mode-conditioned
training --- separate examples with an output-mode tag --- behaves very
differently. At 7B it trails single-modality baselines, but at 32B the
conditioned model's formal mode reaches $85.0\pm8.7$ answer \passk{1},
\emph{21.1 points above formal-only training}, while retaining a usable
natural mode ($77.2\pm11.7$). Raw generations show clean mode separation. A
sufficiently large model can learn formal derivation as an additional mode,
and exposure to natural-language derivations helps rather than hurts its
formal reasoning.

\paragraph{Shortcut structure can invert the effect.}
When a spurious schema marker is planted on 80 percent of training examples
and removed at test time, natural supervision is the robust condition
($78.5\pm14.9$ vs.\ $43.1\pm7.7$ OOD \passk{1}). The formal advantage is not
generic distribution-shift robustness; a model trained on rigid syntax appears
to bind more readily to rigid spurious features as well.

\paragraph{A pretrained base is required.}
Models trained from scratch at 50M--200M parameters on the same corpora fail
at every size and in both conditions (OOD joint \passk{k} of 0.0 for all
$k\le 8$). The lever operates on reasoning competence that pretraining
supplies; it does not conjure it.

Together these results rule out the strong claims in both directions. Formal
traces are not a universal upgrade --- shallow coverage, larger capacity, and
shortcut-laden data all favor prose --- and prose is not a safe default: at
deep coverage it collapses where formal traces extrapolate, and it offers no
verification channel at any scale.

\section{A Preregistered Transfer Study}
\label{sec:transfer}

The controlled study establishes causation on synthetic tasks. The practical
question is whether a small admixture of formal derivations improves ordinary
downstream reasoning. We preregistered a three-way comparison before any
condition completed: midtrain Qwen2.5-7B for five billion tokens on
(a) the Dolmino midtraining mixture~\citep{olmo2025olmo2} alone,
(b) Dolmino with 5 percent of tokens replaced by natural-language synthetic
derivations, and (c) with the same fraction replaced by the paired formal
derivations. The injected corpora contain identical latent derivations,
prompts, and answers in a shared modality-neutral document envelope; the
realized 95:5 token split is audited exactly at every checkpoint. All three
checkpoints then receive \emph{identical} instruction tuning (full-parameter
SFT on a frozen 100K-example Dolci subset, one epoch, shared learning rate
chosen once for all conditions, following the Tulu-3
recipe~\citep{lambert2024tulu3}), fixing the response interface before
evaluation. The preregistered readouts are a ten-task macro (GSM8K, MATH-500,
ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, PIQA, LogiQA, BBH, MMLU, MMLU-Pro) and
a multi-hop macro over context-provided HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and
MuSiQue~\citep{yang2018hotpotqa,ho2020constructing,trivedi2022musique} at a
32{,}768-token window, with a mandatory inspection of retained generations
before any aggregate difference is accepted. Appendix~\ref{app:transfer}
gives the full protocol and per-task tables.

\paragraph{Replacing five percent of a curated mixture is free.}
The ten-task macros are 59.0, 58.9, and 58.6 for control, formal, and natural
respectively (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}): substituting one twentieth of a
production midtraining mixture with synthetic derivations of either kind
costs nothing on standard benchmarks. For practitioners considering synthetic
reasoning admixtures, neutrality of the substitution is itself a useful
datum.

\paragraph{What transfers is response control, not (yet) reasoning.}
On the tagged multi-hop protocol both trace conditions appear to deliver
exactly the hoped-for result --- formal exceeds both control ($+7.7$ points,
95\% CI $[+4.6,+10.8]$) and matched natural ($+2.6$, $[+0.7,+4.5]$).
The preregistered inspection gate reveals what the aggregate hides. The
control model usually \emph{answers correctly but ignores the instructed
\texttt{<answer>} format} (50.7 percent compliance vs.\ 83.8/80.8 for the
trace conditions), and untagged answers score zero. Conditioned on both
models producing an extractable answer, every pairwise difference collapses
(formal$-$natural $+0.8$\,[$-0.8,+2.3$]); rescoring all generations with a
compliance-robust extractor eliminates the gaps entirely (bottom row of
Table~\ref{tab:transfer}). The stock-prompt protocol is null throughout. The
honest summary: at this scale, midtraining on structured derivations
transfers \emph{instructed answer formatting} --- a real but modest
behavioral effect, with formal marginally ahead of natural --- and does not
transfer measurable multi-hop reasoning.

\paragraph{Why the null is informative.}
The controlled study predicts the formal advantage only under intact deep
derivations (Section~\ref{sec:main-results}). A post-hoc audit of the
training pipeline shows the fixed-window packing loader split 44.0 percent of
formal and 47.0 percent of natural derivation documents across 4{,}096-token
windows, training on proof fragments whose opening premises or closing
answers lie outside the window. The pipeline therefore removed the deep
intact structure on which the causal effect depends --- the synthetic
crossover and the transfer null are two observations of the same coverage
condition, not a contradiction. Document-preserving training runs that repair
this defect are in progress; we report the present result as the honest
outcome of the preregistered protocol.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Representation is a training variable.}
The paired experiments show that two renderings of the same derivation are
not interchangeable supervision. The trace language changes the
generalization regime a model enters, with a sign flip governed by depth
coverage --- a variable invisible in any single-corpus experiment. Studies of
reasoning data that vary content while leaving representation uncontrolled
are therefore missing a lever that can swing OOD accuracy by a factor of
four.

\paragraph{Checkability compounds the gain.}
The verifier result reframes why formal traces are attractive. Even where
formal supervision loses at \passk{1} (as at 32B), it retains near-ceiling
verified coverage, and an answer-blind checker converts that coverage into
top-1 accuracy at inference time. Natural-language traces offer no analogous
channel: their failure is often an unfinished, uncheckable trace. This
asymmetry is exactly the interface that verifiable-reward posttraining
needs, and our checkpoints and checker make the corresponding RL experiment
directly runnable; we leave its outcome as an explicitly open question rather
than an assumption.

\paragraph{The path to transfer runs through data engineering.}
The transfer study is a cautionary template. Its apparent multi-hop gain
would have survived casual review --- it was statistically significant on the
preregistered macro --- yet decomposition attributes it to answer formatting.
We suspect effects of this kind are widespread in midtraining ablations
evaluated through format-sensitive extractors. Conversely, the diagnosed
packing defect gives a concrete, testable route to genuine transfer:
preserve derivations intact, cover depth, then re-run the same preregistered
comparison.

\section{Limitations}

The causal comparison relies on synthetic tasks; this control buys attribution
at the cost of narrower semantics than open-domain reasoning. The large
\branchproof{} gain requires deep training coverage and is not reproduced as a
\passk{1} advantage at every capacity, and on \attrcon{} the joint-validity
comparison favors the natural condition. Natural-language validity is
measured through a task-specific translator, whereas formal validity is
checked directly; answer-only metrics are reported to bound any translator
effect. The 32B conditioned comparison changes capacity and data exposure
together. The transfer study uses one base-model family, one mixture rate,
one training run per condition (uncertainty is example-level), and a packing
pipeline whose document-splitting defect we identified only post hoc; the
repaired preregistered rerun is the decisive test, not this one. We report
verifier selection at test time and make no claim that its gains transfer to
\passk{1} under reinforcement learning.

\section{Conclusion}

Holding problems, answers, and latent derivations fixed while changing only
the language of the reasoning trace changes what a language model learns to
do. With sufficient depth coverage, formal traces quadruple
out-of-distribution multi-hop accuracy over matched natural-language traces,
and their machine-checkability converts sampled coverage into near-ceiling
top-1 accuracy without any answer signal. The effect has sharp boundary
conditions --- coverage, capacity, and shortcut structure all modulate it ---
and our preregistered midtraining study shows that transferring it to
general-purpose training is a data-engineering problem whose first identified
requirement, intact deep derivations, was violated by standard packing
practice. Trace representation deserves the same experimental attention as
trace content: it is a lever, it is cheap to pull, and it is currently being
set by convention rather than by measurement.

\subsection{Training and instruction tuning}

All three conditions midtrain Qwen2.5-7B for 9{,}537 steps at global batch
128 and sequence length 4{,}096 (5{,}000{,}134{,}656 tokens) with a shared
learning-rate schedule (peak $10^{-5}$, 256 warmup steps, cosine decay).
The injected corpora replace 5 percent of Dolmino tokens; the realized split
is audited to 95:5 exactly at every saved checkpoint, and both trace corpora
use the same modality-neutral envelope
(\texttt{\{problem\} Solution: \{trace\} Final answer: \{answer\}}) with no
modality tags. Instruction tuning is identical across conditions:
full-parameter SFT on a frozen 100K-example subset of Dolci-Instruct-SFT,
native chat template, assistant-only loss, effective batch 128, learning rate
$5\times10^{-6}$ fixed once for all conditions to avoid treatment-specific
hyperparameter selection.

\subsection{Decomposition analysis}

For every tagged multi-hop example we record whether each condition produced
an extractable answer, and we rescore all generations with a fallback
extractor that accepts the first response line when no tag is present.
Restricted to examples where both compared conditions produce an extractable
answer, pooled paired deltas are: formal$-$natural $+0.8$\,[$-0.8,+2.3$],
formal$-$control $-0.9$\,[$-3.8,+1.8$], natural$-$control
$-2.5$\,[$-5.3,+0.3$]. Under the fallback rescorer the macros are 33.9, 35.4,
and 34.3 (control/formal/natural) with all pairwise intervals covering zero.
Inspection of control-condition failures confirms the mechanism: the model
emits a correct bare answer (for example \texttt{Long Island}) with no
\texttt{<answer>} wrapper and receives an F1 of zero under strict tagged
extraction.
