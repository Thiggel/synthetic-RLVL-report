\title{Proofs from a Program: Teacher-Free Derivations as Midtraining Data,\\and the Depth at Which Formal Notation Wins}

\author{Anonymous authors}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Synthetic pretraining data is almost always written by a language model, which
bounds it by what that model knows and passes on its errors. We study a source
with no model in it: a program samples a multi-hop deduction problem, solves
it, and writes the solution out, so every document is correct by construction.
Replacing ten percent of a 2.5 billion token midtraining mixture for
Qwen2.5-7B with such derivations raises accuracy on held-out ProofWriter
problems from 44.4 to 57.0 percent at inference depth 3. Replacing the same
share with ordinary documents of the same length distribution leaves it at
45.4 percent. The midtrained models never reproduce the generator's notation,
and their scores on general benchmarks stay within sampling error of the
control. The same latent proof
can be written as a formal derivation or as controlled English, and we generate
both from one source so that only the notation differs. Fine-tuning OLMo-3-7B
on either side of the pair, English supervision generalizes further when
training proofs are at most 20 hops deep; at 25 hops the ordering reverses,
with formal supervision at $75.0\pm9.5$ percent out-of-distribution accuracy
against $17.8\pm4.7$ and $99.6\pm0.3$ percent within sixteen samples against
$42.5\pm5.8$. Because a formal derivation is checkable, a proof checker selects
a correct sample without an answer key, at $97.3\pm1.1$ percent single-answer
accuracy against $33.8\pm1.4$ for English traces. In the midtraining mixture,
where derivations are a small fraction of the data, English transfers at least
as well as formal. The formal advantage is a property of dense, deep
supervision, which is also the regime in which a model's reasoning can be
verified, and we argue it is the regime worth training toward.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The public supply of human-written text is finite, and current projections
place its exhaustion for frontier training runs within this decade
\citep{villalobos2024position}. The standard remedy is synthetic text, and in
nearly every published recipe a language model writes it: textbook-style
corpora \citep{gunasekar2023textbooks, eldan2023tinystories}, rephrased web
documents \citep{maini2024rephrasing, maini2025beyondweb, su2025nemotroncc},
and expansions of a small domain corpus by prompting \citep{yang2025synthetic}.
Such data inherits the generating model's errors and blind spots, and training
on it repeatedly narrows the distribution
\citep{shumailov2024collapse, dohmatob2025strong}. Verified synthetic data
avoids this in theory \citep{feng2025verification}, and a few systems have
trained on program-generated, mechanically checked derivations at scale
\citep{trinh2024alphageometry}. Whether such data belongs in the pretraining
mixture of a general language model has not been tested under controls.

A few hundred lines of Python can sample a multi-hop deduction problem, solve
it, and write the solution out. The result is correct by construction,
unlimited in quantity, and free of any teacher. It is also narrow and unlike
the web. We test whether it is worth including. Replacing ten percent of a 2.5
billion token midtraining mixture for Qwen2.5-7B \citep{qwen2025qwen25} with
program-generated derivations raises accuracy on held-out ProofWriter problems
\citep{tafjord2021proofwriter} from 44.4 to 57.0 percent at inference depth 3
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer-pw}). Replacing the same ten percent with long
documents from the same corpus, matched to the derivations in length
distribution, gives 45.4 percent. The gain is confined to items that require
inference, it survives a correction for label bias, and none of the model's
1{,}000 greedy generations on the near-transfer items reproduces the
generator's notation, so the model retained the deduction and not the format.

A latent proof can be rendered as a formal derivation with numbered lines and
named inference rules, or as controlled English, and which rendering the
program should write is the second question. We render every proof both ways from one source, so
that the prompt, the answer and the proof graph are identical within a pair and
only the notation differs (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). Models trained on the
two sides of the pair are as close to a controlled comparison as training data
permits. Prior synthetic deduction corpora render their proofs in English only
\citep{morishita2023fld, morishita2024alt}, and the one study that compared
symbolic and English supervision on matched problems found English to
generalize better \citep{zhou2025finelogic}.

We find that both orderings occur, and that training depth decides between
them. Fine-tuning OLMo-3-7B \citep{olmo2025olmo2} on 50{,}000 paired examples
per condition, English supervision generalizes further when training proofs
reach at most 20 hops. At 25 hops the ordering reverses, with formal
supervision at $75.0\pm9.5$ percent out-of-distribution accuracy against
$17.8\pm4.7$ (Table~\ref{tab:crossover}). Sixteen samples instead of one move
the reversal to 15 hops, so formal supervision widens the set of problems the
model can solve before it improves the first attempt, and because a formal
derivation is checkable, a proof checker turns that wider set into
$97.3\pm1.1$ percent single-answer accuracy with no answer key, against
$33.8\pm1.4$ for English traces. The reversal is not unconditional. A spurious
marker planted in training removes it, and a 32B model prefers English on the
first attempt while keeping formal coverage at 98.8 percent.

In the midtraining mixture the derivations are a tenth of the data and the
model is instruction-tuned afterwards, and there the English rendering
transfers at least as well as the formal one, 57.0 against 50.0 percent on
ProofWriter at depth 3. Read together, the three settings say that the formal
advantage is a property of dense, deep supervision: a small dose of formal
traces is learned less thoroughly than the same dose of prose. The dense regime
is also the only one in which a trained model's reasoning can be verified by a
checker, and verifiable-reward training converts sampled coverage into
first-attempt accuracy \citep{yue2025does}, so we argue it is the regime worth
training toward.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Synthetic data for pretraining.}
Small models pretrained on generated textbooks reach far above their size
\citep{gunasekar2023textbooks, eldan2023tinystories}, rephrased web text speeds
pretraining severalfold \citep{maini2024rephrasing}, and the recipe has been
scaled to trillions of tokens \citep{maini2025beyondweb, su2025nemotroncc} and
into production mixtures \citep{abdin2024phi4, benallal2025smollm2,
olmo2025olmo2}. Continued pretraining on generated expansions of a small
corpus gives log-linear gains \citep{yang2025synthetic}, and the gains from
synthetic data are largest at modest mixing fractions
\citep{kang2025demystifying}. All of these use a language model as the
generator. Training on model output degrades the tail of the distribution
\citep{shumailov2024collapse, dohmatob2024tale, alemohammad2024mad}, at
fractions as low as one percent in the scaling regime
\citep{dohmatob2025strong}, unless real data accumulates alongside
\citep{gerstgrasser2024collapse} or a verifier filters the synthetic samples
\citep{feng2025verification}. Our generator is a program with a built-in
checker, so its output cannot carry a model's errors.

\paragraph{Procedural data without a generator model.}
Pretraining on structured non-linguistic data transfers to language
\citep{papadimitriou2020music, chiang2022transferability}, a large share of
pretraining's benefit is reproduced by trivially simple synthetic tasks
\citep{wu2022insights, krishna2021nonsense}, and pre-pretraining on formal
languages lowers natural-language loss at a smaller token budget
\citep{hu2025circuits}. Program-generated, verified geometry proofs have
trained an olympiad-level prover with no human data
\citep{trinh2024alphageometry}, and program-generated grade-school math with
controlled dependency depth teaches a reasoning skill that generalizes to
deeper problems \citep{ye2025physics21}. Influence functions attribute
reasoning to procedural documents in pretraining \citep{ruis2025procedural},
midtraining helps most for domains far from web prose
\citep{liu2025midtraining}, and upsampling reasoning-dense data late in
training yields large gains at small cost \citep{blakeney2024sparkjoy,
wang2025octothinker}. Procedural generators with verifiers are standard for reinforcement learning
\citep{stojanovski2025reasoninggym, liu2025synlogic, chen2025enigmata,
xie2025logicrl}, and concurrent work supplies procedural corpora for
pretraining \citep{lacombe2026reasoningcore, jiang2026proceduralpretraining}.
None of this work places a program-generated deduction corpus into a
production midtraining mixture under a length-matched control.

\paragraph{Synthetic deduction corpora.}
RuleTaker \citep{clark2020softreasoners} and ProofWriter
\citep{tafjord2021proofwriter} generate rule-and-fact theories and render them
in English, with depth generalization degrading beyond the training range.
PrOntoQA generates problems from a first-order world model so that a chain of
thought can be parsed back into a proof \citep{saparov2023language,
saparov2023ood}. FLD grounds a deduction corpus in a complete set of inference
rules \citep{morishita2023fld}, and additional training on its successor
improves a 70B model on logic, mathematics and code \citep{morishita2024alt}.
Statistical shortcuts can satisfy such corpora in distribution and fail out of
it \citep{zhang2023paradox}. Each of these corpora renders its proofs in
English. Prolog solver traces \citep{feng2024logipt} and Hilbert-style Boolean
proofs \citep{xia2025formallogic} have been used as fine-tuning targets without
a matched English arm. The closest study fine-tunes on one English and three
symbolic supervision styles for the same problems and concludes that English
generalizes better to long chains \citep{zhou2025finelogic}, which matches our
result at training depths up to 20 and reverses at 25.

\paragraph{The language of reasoning traces.}
Intermediate steps improve multi-step accuracy \citep{wei2022chain,
kojima2022large, nye2021show}, rationales are an efficient supervision signal
\citep{hsieh2023distilling, li2023scotd}, and constant-depth transformers need
serial tokens to carry out serial computation \citep{feng2023towards,
merrill2024expressive, li2024chain}. Format governs length generalization
\citep{anil2022exploring, zhou2024rasp, zhou2024notrobustly, hu2024casebased}.
At inference time, writing reasoning as code or as a formal specification and
executing it beats prose \citep{gao2023pal, chen2023program, li2024chainofcode,
ye2023satlm, pan2023logiclm, olausson2023linc, lyu2023faithfulcot}, and the
formal surface helps even without execution \citep{puerto2024codeprompting}.
As training data, program-format solutions beat chain-of-thought for small
math models because programs can be executed and filtered \citep{zhu2024pad,
yue2024mammoth}. Prose rationales can misrepresent what drove an answer
\citep{turpin2023language, lanham2023measuring, chen2025reasoningmodels},
which a checked derivation cannot. None of these studies holds the latent
proof fixed while varying the notation.

\paragraph{Coverage, verification and verifiable rewards.}
Coverage grows with the number of samples and converts into accuracy only where
an automatic verifier exists \citep{brown2024large}, with verifiers trained
\citep{cobbe2021training, lightman2024lets} or replaced by agreement
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency}. Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards
mostly sharpens a model toward what it could already sample
\citep{yue2025does, shao2025spurious, agarwal2025entropymin}, supervised
fine-tuning expands the set of correct trajectories where reinforcement
learning compresses the incorrect ones \citep{matsutani2026squeezes}, and
prolonged training can widen coverage again \citep{liu2025prorl,
cheng2025entropyexploration}. In formal theorem proving the checker is the
reward \citep{xin2025deepseekprover, lin2025goedelprover, wang2025kimina,
hubert2025alphaproof}.

\section{Paired Derivations from One Generator}
\label{sec:paired}

Each example begins with a latent directed acyclic proof. The generator emits a
prompt containing ground facts, rules, a query and distractor branches, then
traverses the supporting path to produce the answer. A rendering function
writes that path in one of two notations. The \formal{} rendering declares
constants and predicates and applies named inference rules to numbered lines.
The \nat{} rendering expresses the same substitutions and conclusions, line for
line, in controlled sentences. Both targets end with the same answer field, and
the prompt is English in both conditions.

\branchproof{} composes Horn-style inferences in which every step offers four
locally plausible branches, exactly one derivable from the current state, with
a fresh constant introduced at every layer. The generator computes the full
Horn closure of each example and accepts it only when exactly one candidate
answer is derivable, which an independent solver confirms over 3{,}000
instances (Appendix~\ref{sec:uniqueness-audit}). The branching factor is 4 and
the distractor ratio 0.5 in every experiment, depths are sampled round-robin,
and corpus seeds are disjoint across training, evaluation and sampling.
Appendices~\ref{sec:grammar} to~\ref{sec:paired-example} give the grammar, the
generator, the validators and complete paired targets.

The controlled study fine-tunes OLMo-3-7B \citep{olmo2025olmo2} with LoRA on
50{,}000 examples per condition, with maximum training depth $D \in
\{5,10,15,20,25\}$ and three seeds per cell. Evaluation covers depths 1 to 50
with 32 prompts per depth, sixteen samples at temperature 1.0 per prompt and one
greedy decode, and the unbiased \passk{k} estimator \citep{chen2021evaluating}.
Depths above $D$ are out of distribution. Both notations receive the same
7{,}168-token generation limit inside a 16{,}384-token window, and a tokenizer
audit confirms that every gold target fits in both renderings. Joint metrics
require a correct answer and a mechanically valid proof, with formal traces
checked directly and English traces checked after a deterministic translation
into the same engine. Appendices~\ref{sec:training-details}
and~\ref{sec:metrics} give the configuration and the metric definitions.

\passk{1} crosses at $D=25$ and \passk{16} at $D=15$, where the \formal{} condition
reaches $69.9$ percent against $56.9$. Between the two crossings the formally
supervised model can construct a correct derivation within sixteen attempts but
not on the first, so the set of solvable problems widens ten depth levels
before single-sample reliability improves. A study reporting greedy accuracy
alone would describe $D=15$ and $D=20$ as showing no formal advantage. The two
conditions also respond to deeper training proofs in opposite directions:
\nat{} \passk{16} falls from $73.5$ percent at $D=5$ to $42.5$ at $D=25$, and
\formal{} \passk{16} rises from $50.0$ to $99.6$. Neither notation is better in
general. The ordering flips with training depth, and where it flips depends on
the decoding rule.

\paragraph{Supervision quantity.}
Formal targets are longer than English targets, so the comparison could measure
the number of supervised tokens. Equating supervised target tokens across
conditions by subsampling gives $41.0\pm20.1$ percent \passk{1} and
$69.8\pm34.9$ percent \passk{16} for the token-matched \nat{} condition at
$D=25$ (Table~\ref{tab:controls}), against $99.6\pm0.3$ \passk{16} for
\formal{}. The ordering is unchanged. The variance of this control is large,
and we report it as bounding the explanation and not as a precise estimate.

\subsection{A checker selects the correct sample without labels}
\label{sec:verifier}

A model whose sixteen samples contain a correct proof is only useful if that
proof can be found without the answer. For formal derivations it can, since
validity is decided by the same engine that generated the problem. Drawing
sixteen samples, checking each in order and returning the answer of the first
one the checker accepts gives $97.3\pm1.1$ percent single-answer accuracy at
$D=25$, against a joint \passk{16} ceiling of $98.1\pm0.5$ percent. The $0.8$
point gap is the cost of derivations the checker accepts that reach a wrong
answer. No reward model and no gold labels enter the procedure. The same
selector applied to translated \nat{} traces recovers $33.8\pm1.4$ percent,
mainly because long English derivations fail to terminate inside the
generation budget, so there is no checkable derivation to accept.

\paragraph{Shortcuts.}
A spurious marker that identifies the active branch on 80 percent of training
examples and is removed at test time \citep{geirhos2020shortcut} reverses the
comparison. The \nat{} condition is barely affected, at $99.4\pm0.9$ percent
\passk{16} and $76.1$ percent at depth 50, while the \formal{} condition falls
to $87.3\pm7.5$ and to $11.7$ percent at depth 50. The formal advantage is
therefore not a general robustness to distribution shift. A model trained on
rigid syntax binds more readily to a rigid spurious feature.

\paragraph{Both notations in one model.}
Concatenating the two renderings into one target fails, at $0.5\pm0.4$ and
$0.2\pm0.1$ percent \passk{1}, because the model copies one or both long traces
and exhausts its budget. Separate examples with an output-mode tag reach
$50.3\pm6.4$ percent \passk{1} in formal mode, below single-notation training.

\paragraph{Base model.}
On Qwen2.5-7B the conditions are within variance of each other, at
$69.9\pm11.1$ against $74.9\pm7.0$ percent \passk{1}. On OLMo-3-32B \nat{}
leads at $98.9\pm0.8$ against $63.9\pm5.1$ percent \passk{1}, while \formal{}
\passk{16} stays at $98.8\pm0.9$. At 32B the formal deficit is one of
probability mass on the first sample, not of coverage. Mode conditioning at 32B
lifts the formal mode to $85.0\pm8.7$ percent \passk{1}. Across the three base
models, \formal{} \passk{16} is at or above \nat{} \passk{16} in every
$D=25$ cell except Qwen2.5-7B, where the two are within one standard deviation.

\section{Midtraining a 7B Model on Derivations from a Program}
\label{sec:transfer}

The controlled study supplies dense supervision on one task, and the practical
question is whether a small share of such data improves an ordinary model when
placed in an ordinary mixture, on benchmarks the generator never saw. We test
this by midtraining Qwen2.5-7B \citep{qwen2025qwen25} on the Dolmino mixture
\citep{olmo2025olmo2} with a fixed fraction replaced.

\subsection{Design}

Five conditions share every hyperparameter, every data path outside the
replaced slice, and a token budget of $2{,}500{,}853{,}760$ training tokens,
reached as 2{,}385 steps of global batch 128 at sequence length 8{,}192. They
differ in the ten percent of loss tokens that is replaced.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \ctrl{} trains on the unmodified mixture.
\item \longdoc{} replaces ten percent with long documents drawn from the same
mixture, selected to match the token-length histogram of the \formal{} corpus.
\item \formal{} replaces ten percent with formal derivations generated at
depths 1 to 25.
\item \nat{} replaces ten percent with the English rendering of the same latent
proofs.
\item \condensed{} replaces ten percent with a formal rendering of the same
latent proofs that states the theory once, 2.6 times shorter.
\end{enumerate}
The \formal{} document states its theory twice, as the English prompt and
again as formal premises, and then gives the proof; the \nat{} document
states the theory once, in English. Formal premises are about 1.5 times more
compact than controlled English for the same theory, so the two documents are
length-matched by that offset, and \condensed{} is the formal document with
the theory stated once.
\longdoc{} separates two explanations of any gain, since derivations are long
documents and long documents change what a model learns about carrying state
across a window. Its median length is $3{,}712$ tokens against $3{,}749$ for
\formal{}, and all thirty populated 250-token bins agree within three percent.
The three derivation corpora contain 72{,}000 documents each and render the
same latent proofs. A document-preserving packer never splits a document
across windows, and a decoded-batch audit refuses to start training if any
window contains a split document or an unmasked padding token; zero documents
were split or excluded in any condition. Because the replaced slice is fixed
in tokens, \condensed{} traverses its proof set 2.31 times where \formal{} and
\nat{} traverse theirs 0.90 and 0.89 times, so its comparison with \nat{} is
one of a compact rendering seen more than twice against a verbose one seen
once (Appendix~\ref{sec:midtrain-details}).

All five checkpoints receive the same instruction tuning on 100{,}000 examples
of a public instruction corpus at seed 3407, and a second replicate at seed
3408 is reported where available. Evaluation uses two families. The external
family is ProofWriter under the open-world assumption at inference depths 0, 1,
2, 3 and 5, with 500 items per depth, which the generator never produced. The
near-transfer family asks for a written derivation on held-out problems from
the generator at depths 5 to 25, with 200 items per depth. A ten-task general
suite and three multi-hop question-answering tasks check for regressions.
Sampled measurements draw sixteen completions at temperature 0.8 on the
prompts logged by the greedy run. Appendix~\ref{sec:midtrain-details} gives
every setting.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:transfer-results}

\paragraph{External transfer.}
At depth 0 of ProofWriter, which requires lookup and no inference, the five
conditions fall between 44.2 and 48.8 percent greedy exact match
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer-pw}). From depth 2 onward \nat{} leads \ctrl{} by 10 to 13
points and \formal{} leads it by 4 to 6 points, and \longdoc{} stays within 2
points of \ctrl{} at every depth. The gain therefore follows from the deduction
in the replaced slice and not from its length, and it appears only where
inference is required.

\centering

\paragraph{Near transfer.}
On held-out problems from the generator, prompted to write a derivation before
answering, \ctrl{} scores between 3.5 and 11.0 percent greedy exact match
across depths and \longdoc{} between 7.5 and 15.0. Every derivation condition
is far above both: \nat{} between 24.5 and 52.5 percent, \formal{} between 17.5
and 31.5, and \condensed{} between 18.5 and 34.5
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer-passk}). The replaced slice teaches a capability
the control mixture does not supply. Under greedy decoding \nat{} is ahead of
\formal{} at every depth, by 3.0 to 21.0 points.

The proportion of greedy generations that reach an answer field is 64.5 to
78.5 percent for \ctrl{}, 79.5 to 92.5 for \longdoc{}, 94.0 to 99.5 for
\formal{}, and 100 for \nat{}, so models that never saw a derivation in
midtraining often stop before concluding.

\paragraph{General benchmarks.}
On nine general benchmarks and three long-context multi-hop tasks under
standard prompting, the five conditions are within 1.3 points of each other on
GSM8K, MATH-500, BBH, MMLU, HellaSwag, WinoGrande and PIQA, with GSM8K between
78.6 and 79.8 percent and MMLU between 69.0 and 69.8
(Table~\ref{tab:general}). The largest spread is on LogiQA, where \ctrl{}
scores 31.2 and the derivation conditions 26.6 to 28.1 on 651 items, and on
2WikiMultihopQA, 34.3 to 38.9 on 200 items, in both cases with \ctrl{} at or
near the top and within two standard errors of the others. Replacing ten
percent of the mixture does not help tasks that do not ask for a derivation,
and any cost on them is at the level of sampling error.

\paragraph{The midtrained models do not write formal notation.}
None of the 1{,}000 greedy near-transfer generations of the \formal{} arm
contains any marker of the training rendering, and the same holds for every
other condition. After instruction tuning all five models answer in prose. The
gain reported above is therefore not an effect of adopting formal notation, and
the checker-based selection of Section~\ref{sec:verifier} does not apply at
this scale, since no formal derivation is emitted for a checker to accept.

\subsection{Statistical status}
\label{sec:transfer-limits}

Each midtraining condition was trained once. An auxiliary experiment on the
same evaluation suite, in which five instruction-tuning conditions were each
trained under two seeds, gives a per-run standard deviation near $0.019$ in
exact match and near $0.027$ for a difference between two independently seeded
conditions (Appendix~\ref{sec:variance}). The contrasts against \ctrl{}, 4 to
13 points on ProofWriter and 12 to 48 points on the near-transfer family, are
several times that. The contrast between \formal{} and \nat{} on ProofWriter,
7 points at depth 3, is about twice it, and we report the ordering of the two
notations at midtraining scale as observed under one seed, with the replicate

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What transfers is the deduction.}
Both renderings raise ProofWriter accuracy, the length-matched control does
not, and the trained models write neither notation. The content of a
program-generated derivation survives instruction tuning while its surface
form does not, which is the property one wants from a synthetic source that
costs nothing to run and cannot carry a generating model's errors.

\paragraph{The formal advantage is a property of dense, deep supervision.}
Across the settings studied, formal supervision wins the first attempt in
exactly one: 50{,}000 examples on a single task, training proofs through 25
hops, on a 7B model. It wins coverage more broadly, at $D \geq 15$ and at 32B.
It loses the first attempt when training proofs are shallow, when a shortcut
is available, when the model is larger, and when the derivations are a tenth
of a midtraining mixture, where it is behind on coverage as well. The formal rendering is further from the pretraining
distribution than controlled English, so a given dose of it is learned less
thoroughly and the model falls back on prose-shaped behaviour on the first
sample. The same dose of English is learned more thoroughly but produces a
narrower distribution over derivations at depth, which is what the falling
\nat{} \passk{16} curve in Table~\ref{tab:crossover} measures.

\paragraph{Why the dense regime is the one to train toward.}
In the dense regime almost every held-out problem has a correct formal
derivation within sixteen samples, at depths beyond the training range and
with distractor branches at every step, and that derivation can be found by a
checker with no labels. A system that samples and verifies wants exactly these
two properties, and prose supervision supplies neither.
Verifiable-reward training principally reweights a model toward completions it
can already sample \citep{yue2025does, matsutani2026squeezes}, so the coverage a
model has after supervised training bounds what that stage can reach, and in
the dense regime formal supervision moves that bound from $42.5$ to $99.6$
percent. Ten percent of a midtraining mixture is not enough to make a model
write derivations it can be checked on. The next step is a larger share, or an
instruction stage that preserves the notation, followed by verifiable-reward
training on both notations. We have not run that stage. Our results establish
that the ingredients it needs are present after dense formal supervision and
absent after prose supervision.

\section{Limitations}

The controlled study uses LoRA fine-tuning on one model family at 7B and 32B
and a second at 7B. The midtraining study uses a third model, trained once per
condition, so the ordering of the two notations at that scale rests on one
seed plus the replicate in the appendix, and the depth thresholds it inherits
were measured on OLMo-3-7B and not on Qwen2.5-7B. The generator produces one
family of Horn-style deduction problems without arithmetic, ambiguity or
retrieval, and the depth axis is proof depth alone. Proof validity for English
traces depends on a deterministic translation into the proof engine that the
formal condition does not need, so answer accuracy is reported alongside every
joint metric. The \condensed{} condition sees each of its proofs 2.6 times more often than the
other two derivation conditions, and ProofWriter's \emph{unknown} class is
unsolved by every model. An earlier
configuration of the midtraining study, at sequence length 4{,}096 with a five
percent share and derivations capped at depth 14, produced no measurable
transfer; it differs in window, share, rendering and evaluation suite and is
not a controlled comparison (Appendix~\ref{sec:earlier-config}).

\section{Conclusion}

A program that writes out its own deductions is a usable source of midtraining
data. At ten percent of a 2.5 billion token mixture it raises held-out
deduction accuracy by up to 13 points, against a length-matched control that
raises nothing, and it leaves general benchmarks unchanged. How the program
writes matters, and the answer depends on how much of its output the model
sees. With dense supervision through 25 hops, formal notation produces a model
that solves nearly every held-out problem within sixteen samples and whose
samples a checker can select without labels. At a tenth of a mixture, English
transfers at least as well. The regime in which formal notation wins is the
regime in which reasoning can be verified, which is a reason to train toward
it.
