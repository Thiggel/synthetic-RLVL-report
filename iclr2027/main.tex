\title{Depth Coverage Determines Whether Formal or\\Natural-Language Reasoning Traces Generalize Better}

\author{Anonymous authors}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Supervised reasoning traces are almost always written in natural language,
though the same derivation can be written as a sequence of explicit rule
applications. We isolate that variable with paired corpora in which the prompt,
the answer and the latent proof are identical across conditions and only the
trace rendering differs. Which rendering generalizes better depends on how deep
the training derivations go. Below a depth threshold natural-language traces
extrapolate further out of distribution, and at maximum training depth 25 the
ordering reverses, with formal supervision at $75.0\pm9.5$ percent
out-of-distribution answer \passk{1} against $17.8\pm4.7$ for the matched
natural condition. The reversal appears ten depth levels earlier under sampling
than under greedy decoding, which separates two thresholds and identifies
coverage, and not single-sample accuracy, as what formal supervision buys
first. Because formal derivations are checkable, that coverage is recoverable
without an answer key, and an answer-blind selector returning the first sample
a proof checker accepts converts sixteen samples into $97.3\pm1.1$ percent
single-answer accuracy against $33.8\pm1.4$ percent for translated
natural-language traces. Midtraining Qwen2.5-7B on 2.5 billion tokens with ten
percent of the mixture replaced by matched derivations at depth 25 reproduces
the dissociation. Both trace conditions improve external deduction accuracy
over an untreated control and over a length-matched corpus carrying no deep
deduction, and the formal condition exceeds the natural condition under
sampling while falling below it under greedy decoding. The single-seed limit of
that last comparison is quantified against measured training-run variance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Chain-of-thought supervision trains language models to emit intermediate steps
before an answer \citep{wei2022chain,kojima2022large}, and almost all such
supervision is written in prose. The convention is uniform enough to hide an
experimental variable, since the same derivation can be rendered as discursive
text or as a numbered sequence of rule applications, and the two renderings
present the learner with different token-level structure. This paper measures
what that choice does to the reasoning a model acquires.

Ordinary corpora cannot answer the question, because two solutions written in
different styles rarely encode the same computation. We therefore generate
paired synthetic corpora in which a deterministic generator samples a latent
proof and renders it either as a compact formal derivation or as a controlled
natural-language explanation. Within a pair the prompt, answer, facts, rules
and proof graph are identical and only the trace language changes
(Figure~\ref{fig:overview}), so differences between models trained on the two
sides are attributable to representation. The study concerns training and not
tool use, since no prover is called at inference time and both conditions
generate under matched token budgets.

\caption{\textbf{Left.} The paired-trace design. Each training example exists in

\label{fig:overview}
\end{figure}

Which trace language generalizes better is not a fixed property of the
representation. With shallow-to-intermediate training ranges the
natural-language condition is reliably stronger out of distribution, and once
the training range reaches depth 25 the comparison reverses by a wide margin,
with formal supervision at $75.0\pm9.5$ percent out-of-distribution answer
\passk{1} against $17.8\pm4.7$ (Figure~\ref{fig:crossover}). Length and
token-budget controls show the gap is not explained by target length or by the
number of supervised tokens, and an independently generated
constraint-propagation task reproduces the correctness advantage.

The location of the reversal depends on decoding. Under greedy decoding the
formal condition overtakes the natural condition only at maximum training depth
25, while under sampling with sixteen draws the crossover has already occurred
at maximum training depth 15, at $69.9$ percent against $56.9$. Formal
supervision buys the ability to produce a correct derivation somewhere in a
modest sample budget well before it buys one on the first attempt, so reporting
greedy accuracy alone would place the threshold ten depth levels too high.

Because a checkable representation lets that sample budget be spent without an
answer key, the gap is practically useful. Nearly every test prompt admits a
correct and mechanically valid formal proof within sixteen samples, at joint
\passk{16} of $98.1\pm0.5$ percent, and selecting the first sample the checker
accepts yields $97.3\pm1.1$ percent single-answer accuracy against
$33.8\pm1.4$ for translated natural-language traces. If reinforcement learning
with verifiable rewards sharpens a base model toward samples it can already
draw without extending its coverage \citep{yue2025does}, then the coverage
measured here is what bounds such posttraining.

Whether any of this survives contact with ordinary language-model training is a
separate question. We midtrain Qwen2.5-7B on 2.5 billion tokens of a production
mixture with ten percent of the loss tokens replaced by matched derivations at
depth 25, holding the token budget and every hyperparameter identical across
conditions and adding a control corpus of long documents length-matched to the
formal traces but carrying no deep deduction. Replacement with either trace
language raises accuracy on a held-out external deduction benchmark, the
length-matched control does not reproduce that gain, and the formal condition
exceeds the natural condition under sampling while falling below it under
greedy decoding.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Reasoning traces and controlled synthetic study.}
Chain-of-thought prompting and supervised rationales improve multi-step
reasoning \citep{wei2022chain,kojima2022large}, and later work bootstraps
rationales \citep{zelikman2022star}, decomposes questions
\citep{zhou2023least}, searches over reasoning states \citep{yao2023tree} and
supervises scratchpads \citep{nye2021show}. The benefit concentrates in
mathematical and symbolic domains \citep{sprague2025tocot}. Synthetic corpora
with known generative structure make mechanism questions answerable that
benchmark accuracy cannot address, as in the iGSM family
\citep{ye2024physics,ye2024physics22} and in work controlling the generating
grammar directly \citep{allenzhu2023physics1}. Formal analysis of
chain-of-thought on synthetic deduction exposes failure modes that benchmark
scores hide \citep{saparov2023language}, compositional tasks expose limits that
scale does not remove \citep{dziri2023faith}, the learning-to-reason objective
can be satisfied by statistical shortcuts \citep{zhang2023paradox}, and the
locality of the training distribution has been proposed as the reason
intermediate steps help at all \citep{prystawski2023why}. We adopt the same
instrument and add the representation axis, holding the problem distribution
fixed and varying only the rendering of the supervised trace.

\paragraph{Programs, formal systems and autoformalization.}
Program-aided prompting uses executable code for intermediate computation
\citep{gao2023pal,chen2023program}, logic pipelines delegate inference to an
external solver \citep{pan2023logiclm,ling2023deductive}, rule-based deduction
over language has been studied with generated proof corpora
\citep{clark2020transformers,tafjord2021proofwriter}, theorem proving trains
models to construct machine-checkable objects
\citep{polu2020generative,jiang2022draft,leandojo2023,leanCopilot2024},
autoformalization translates informal statements into such systems
\citep{wu2022autoformalization}, and recent proposals verify traces by typing
them \citep{perrier2025typed}. Those methods intervene at inference time and
change the mechanism producing or validating an answer. Our intervention is at
training time, the inference mechanism is unchanged, and every formal target
has a paired natural-language target from the same proof, so representation is
measurable without confounding it with the availability of a solver.

\paragraph{Verification, selection and test-time compute.}
Trained verifiers rerank sampled solutions \citep{cobbe2021training}, process
supervision scores individual steps \citep{lightman2024lets}, self-consistency
uses sampled agreement \citep{wang2023selfconsistency}, and repeated sampling
converts inference compute into coverage \citep{brown2024large}. How to spend a
fixed test-time budget has been studied empirically
\citep{snell2024scaling,muennighoff2025s1} and theoretically
\citep{setlur2025scaling}. The selector used here needs no trained verifier and
no answer key, since validity of a formal derivation is decidable by the engine
that generated the task. Whether verifiable-reward training expands the
reasoning boundary or sharpens the sampling distribution is disputed
\citep{yue2025does,guo2025deepseekr1,shao2024deepseekmath,gandhi2025cognitive},
and the coverage measurements here speak to the quantity at issue.

\paragraph{Faithfulness and controlled generalization.}
Rationales can omit the factors driving a prediction and can rationalize an
answer produced otherwise \citep{turpin2023language,lanham2023measuring}, so
answer accuracy and proof validity are reported separately throughout.
Synthetic benchmarks control train and test distributions to expose failures of
systematic generalization
\citep{lake2018generalization,keysers2020measuring,hupkes2020compositionality},
with length a distinct axis \citep{anil2022exploring}, and theory shows
chain-of-thought lets a constant-depth transformer emulate serial computation
it otherwise cannot perform
\citep{feng2023towards,merrill2024expressive,li2024chain}. Our shift increases
proof depth while holding the rule system fixed, and a spurious-marker shift
tests shortcut reliance \citep{geirhos2020shortcut}.

\section{A Paired-Trace Testbed}
\label{sec:paired}

Each example begins with a latent directed acyclic proof. A generator emits a
prompt containing ground facts, rules, a query and distractor branches, then
traverses the supporting path to produce the answer. A rendering function
writes that path in one of two trace languages. The \formal{} rendering
declares constants and predicates and applies named inference rules to numbered
lines. The \nat{} rendering expresses the same substitutions and conclusions,
line for line, in controlled sentences. Both targets end with the same answer
field and the prompt is natural language in both conditions.

\branchproof{} composes Horn-style inferences in which every step offers four
locally plausible branches, exactly one derivable from the current state, with
a fresh constant introduced at every layer. The generator computes the full
Horn closure of each example and accepts it only when exactly one candidate
answer is derivable, which removes any reward for guessing among defensible
answers. \attrcon{} propagates values among object attributes under generated
constraints, and its vocabulary and proof construction are disjoint from
\branchproof{}. Generation parameters are held fixed across every controlled
experiment, at branching factor 4 and distractor ratio 0.5, with depths sampled
round-robin. Corpus seeds are disjoint across training, evaluation and sampling
roles. Appendices~\ref{sec:paired-example} to~\ref{sec:generator-eval} give
paired targets, the grammar, the generator and the validators, and
Appendix~\ref{sec:uniqueness-audit} reports the answer-uniqueness validation.

The controlled experiment fine-tunes OLMo-3-7B \citep{olmo2025olmo2} with LoRA
on 50{,}000 materialized examples per condition, with maximum training depth in
$\{5,10,15,20,25\}$ and three seeds per condition. Evaluation covers depths
$\{1,2,5,10,12,15,18,20,25,30,35,40,45,50\}$ with 32 prompts per depth, and at
each prompt sixteen samples at temperature 1.0 together with one greedy decode.
Sampled coverage uses the unbiased \passk{k} estimator
\citep{chen2021evaluating}. Both trace languages receive an identical
7{,}168-token generation limit inside a 16{,}384-token window, and a tokenizer
audit confirms every gold prompt and target fits in both renderings, so neither
condition is truncated. Depths above the maximum training depth are out of
distribution. Joint metrics require the correct answer together with a
mechanically valid proof, where formal traces are checked directly by the proof
engine and natural traces by a deterministic translation into the same engine,
so answer-only accuracy is reported alongside every joint metric.
Appendices~\ref{sec:training-details} and~\ref{sec:metrics} give the remaining
configuration and the metric definitions.

The two decoding rules do not place the reversal at the same depth. Greedy
accuracy and \passk{1} both cross at maximum training depth 25, while
\passk{16} crosses at maximum training depth 15, where the formal condition
reaches $0.699$ against $0.569$. The interval between the two crossings is a
regime in which formal supervision has already produced a model that can
construct a correct derivation within sixteen attempts, and has not yet
produced one that does so on the first attempt. Coverage is acquired before
single-sample reliability. A study reporting greedy accuracy alone would locate
the effect ten depth levels too high and would describe the intermediate range
as showing no formal advantage at all.

The natural condition degrades monotonically in \passk{16} from $0.735$ at
depth 5 to $0.425$ at depth 25 while the formal condition rises monotonically
from $0.500$ to $0.996$, so the two renderings respond to increasing depth
coverage in opposite directions.

\paragraph{Length and budget controls.}
Formal and natural targets differ in length, which raises the possibility that
the comparison measures supervision quantity. Two controls address this. Token
budgets at generation are identical by construction, and the tokenizer audit
confirms no truncation in either condition. Matched-token training, in which
the number of supervised target tokens is equated across conditions, leaves the
ordering unchanged. Details are in Appendix~\ref{sec:length-controls}.

\paragraph{Replication on an independent task.}
\attrcon{} shares no vocabulary and no proof construction with \branchproof{}.
The correctness advantage of formal supervision at high training depth
reproduces there, which makes it unlikely that the effect depends on a
particular rule system.

\section{The Verifiability Dividend}
\label{sec:verifier}

Coverage is useful only if it can be harvested without the answer. For formal
traces it can, since validity is decidable by the engine that generated the
task. Consider a selector that draws $k$ samples, checks each in order and
returns the answer of the first one the checker accepts.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:selector}
Let each sample be, independently, valid and correct with probability $a$,
valid and incorrect with probability $b$, and rejected with probability
$1-a-b$. The first-valid selector returns a correct answer with probability
$\bigl(1-(1-a-b)^{k}\bigr)\,a/(a+b)$. If the checker admits no incorrect proof,
so that $b=0$, this equals $1-(1-a)^{k}$, the joint \passk{k}.
\end{proposition}

The proof is elementary and appears in Appendix~\ref{sec:proof}. Two measurable
quantities therefore bound selection, joint \passk{k} from above and checker
soundness $a/(a+b)$ independently of $k$. On \branchproof{} at maximum training
depth 25 the formal condition attains joint \passk{16} of $98.1\pm0.5$ percent
and the selector attains $97.3\pm1.1$ percent, so the $0.8$ point gap is the
empirical cost of $b>0$ and the selector sits close to the permitted ceiling.

The same selector applied to translated natural-language traces recovers
$33.8\pm1.4$ percent. The deficit is not primarily a failure of translation.
Long natural traces frequently fail to terminate inside the generation budget,
so no checkable derivation exists to accept, which drives $a+b$ down and leaves
the selector with nothing to select from. No reward model and no gold labels
enter the procedure.

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:boundary}

Four further experiments bound where the effect holds, and
Appendix~\ref{sec:boundary-details} reports them in full. Capacity reorders the
comparison, since the two conditions are close on Qwen2.5-7B at $69.9\pm11.1$
against $74.9\pm7.0$ \passk{1} and the natural condition leads on OLMo-3-32B at
$98.9\pm0.8$ against $63.9\pm5.1$, where formal coverage nonetheless stays near
ceiling at $98.8\pm0.9$ \passk{16}. Concatenating both renderings into one
target fails outright, below one percent, while mode-conditioned training at
32B lifts the formal mode to $85.0\pm8.7$, which remains below single-modality
prose at that scale. A spurious schema marker planted on 80 percent of training
examples and removed at test time makes the natural condition the robust one,
at $78.5\pm14.9$ against $43.1\pm7.7$, so the formal advantage is not generic
robustness to distribution shift. Models trained from scratch at 50M to 200M
parameters fail in both conditions at every size, so the effect operates on
competence supplied by pretraining and does not create it.

\section{Transfer to Language-Model Midtraining}
\label{sec:transfer}

Whether the same variable matters when derivations are a small minority of an
ordinary training mixture is tested by midtraining Qwen2.5-7B
\citep{qwen2025qwen25} on a production corpus with a fixed fraction replaced by
matched derivations, on a different model family and against benchmarks the
generator never saw.

\subsection{Design}

Five conditions share every hyperparameter, every data path outside the
replaced slice, and an identical token budget of $2{,}500{,}853{,}760$ training
tokens, reached as 2{,}385 steps of global batch 128 at sequence length 8{,}192.
The conditions differ only in the ten percent of loss tokens that is replaced.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Control} replaces nothing and trains on the unmodified mixture.
\item \textbf{LongDoc} replaces ten percent with long documents drawn from the
same production corpus, selected to match the token-length histogram of the
formal traces and carrying no deep deduction.
\item \textbf{Logic} replaces ten percent with formal derivations at depth 25.
\item \textbf{Natural} replaces ten percent with the natural-language rendering
of the same latent proofs.
\item \textbf{Condensed} replaces ten percent with a compact formal rendering of
the same latent proofs.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{LongDoc} separates two explanations of any observed gain, since formal
derivations are long and long documents change what a model learns about
maintaining state across a context. Matching the length histogram while
removing the deduction isolates the second factor. Median length is $3{,}712$
tokens against $3{,}750$ for the formal traces, mean $3{,}832$ against
$3{,}856$, maximum $7{,}750$ against $7{,}728$, and all thirty populated
250-token bins agree within about three percent.

\subsection{Corpus construction and packing}

A corpus of 72{,}000 examples is generated with depths 1 to 25 round-robin,
branching factor 4 and distractor ratio 0.5, matching the controlled study. The
three trace corpora render the same latent proofs, so the comparison between
them holds the underlying computation fixed as in Section~\ref{sec:paired}.
Rendered lengths and packing statistics appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:midtrain-details}.

Packing preserves documents. A document longer than one window is excluded and
counted, and none is split across windows, so the mid-derivation truncation
that would destroy the depth structure under study cannot occur. Zero documents
were excluded and zero split in every condition. Padding uses a dedicated token
masked out of the loss. A decoded-batch audit verifies both properties on real
loader windows and training refuses to start if it fails.

Because the replaced slice is fixed in tokens and the condensed rendering is
2.6 times shorter, the condensed condition traverses its proof set 2.31 times
where Logic and Natural traverse theirs 0.90 and 0.89 times. Any comparison
involving the condensed condition therefore confounds rendering with exposure,
and we treat it accordingly in Section~\ref{sec:transfer-results}.

\subsection{Instruction tuning and evaluation}

All five checkpoints receive an identical instruction-tuning stage of one epoch
over 100{,}000 examples at learning rate $5\times10^{-6}$, full-parameter under
FSDP. Evaluation uses two families. The external family is ProofWriter
\citep{tafjord2021proofwriter} under the open-world assumption at inference
depths 0, 1, 2, 3 and 5 with 500 items per depth, which the generator never
saw. The near-transfer family asks for a derivation on held-out problems from
the same generator at depths 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25 with 200 items per depth. The
metric is exact match on the extracted answer. Sampled measurements draw
sixteen completions at temperature 0.8 and top-$p$ 0.95, replaying the greedy
prompts so the two decodings differ in the sampler alone.
Appendix~\ref{sec:midtrain-details} gives every remaining setting.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:transfer-results}

On the external ProofWriter items, both trace conditions improve over Control
and over LongDoc, and LongDoc does not improve over Control. At inference depth
3 the ordering is Control $0.444$, LongDoc $0.454$, Logic $0.500$, Natural
$0.570$. At depth 0, which requires lookup and no inference, the conditions are
close together at $0.442$ to $0.488$, which is the shape a genuine deduction
effect should have. The gain therefore does not follow from exposure to long
documents.

Under greedy decoding the natural condition is ahead of the formal condition on
thirteen of fifteen tasks. Under sampling the ordering reverses.

Section~\ref{sec:main-results}, now on a different model family with the

generations reaching a parseable answer is $0.645$ to $0.785$ for Control and

The condensed condition attains the largest gain over Control and exceeds the
formal condition by $0.050$. Its documents are 2.6 times shorter, so its
advantage is not a length effect. It traverses its proof set 2.6 times more
often, so its advantage is confounded with exposure and cannot be attributed to
the rendering. Resolving that requires an exposure-matched condensed corpus,
which is not part of this work.

\subsection{What the transfer result does and does not establish}
\label{sec:transfer-limits}

Every midtraining condition was trained once, so the intervals in
Table~\ref{tab:transfer-boot} estimate evaluation sampling error and not the
variability that retraining under a different seed would produce. That second
quantity is measurable. An auxiliary experiment on the same evaluation suite,
described in Appendix~\ref{sec:variance}, gives a per-run standard deviation
near $0.019$ and therefore near $0.027$ for a difference between two
independently seeded conditions. Combined with the evaluation term this gives
about $0.033$ for the formal against natural contrast, whose interval then
becomes approximately $[-0.015, +0.115]$ and includes zero.

The consequence is specific. The contrasts against Control, between $0.176$ and
$0.276$, are six to ten times the training standard deviation and are not at
risk from single-seed training. The contrast between the two trace languages,
at $0.050$, is roughly twice that standard deviation and is a sign reversal
against the greedy ordering, which is the pattern most likely to be produced by
run-to-run variation. We therefore report the transfer-scale formal advantage
as consistent with the controlled study and not as independently established.
Replication under a second instruction-tuning seed is the appropriate remedy,
since enlarging the evaluation set would shrink the smaller error term and
leave the larger untouched.

The depth of the replaced derivations was fixed at 25 in every condition, so
the transfer study locates no threshold of its own and inherits the one
estimated from OLMo-3-7B in Section~\ref{sec:main-results}. An earlier
configuration at sequence length 4{,}096, with a five percent replacement share
and derivations capped at depth 14, produced no measurable transfer. It differs
from the present configuration in window, replacement share, rendering and
evaluation suite, so it motivates this design and is not a controlled
below-threshold comparison.

\section{Discussion}

Trace language is a training-time variable whose sign depends on depth
coverage. Neither rendering is better in general, and the location of the
threshold depends on how the model is decoded. A study reporting greedy
accuracy alone places the crossover at depth 25 and concludes that formal
supervision is harmful everywhere below it, where the same experiment read at
\passk{16} places it at depth 15 and shows the formal condition ahead through
the upper half of the range. The two readings measure different things, and the
second is the one relevant to any system drawing more than one sample.

That distinction connects these measurements to verifiable-reward
posttraining. If such training principally reweights toward completions the
base model can already produce \citep{yue2025does}, then coverage after
midtraining bounds what the later stage reaches. Trace language moves that
coverage by between $0.050$ and $0.276$ \passk{16} in our transfer setting,
placing it upstream of the posttraining stage. Verifying the ordering requires
running that stage on both trace conditions, which we have not done.

\section{Limitations}

The controlled study uses one model family at 7B and one at 32B, and the
transfer study uses a third. Depth thresholds estimated on OLMo-3-7B are
applied to Qwen2.5-7B by assumption and not by measurement.

The transfer conditions were trained once each, with the consequences set out
in Section~\ref{sec:transfer-limits}.

Proof validity for natural-language traces is established through a
deterministic translation into the proof engine. The translation is a component
that the formal condition does not require, and although answer-only accuracy
is reported alongside every joint metric, the joint natural-language numbers
carry that dependency.

The generator produces one family of Horn-style deduction problems and one
constraint-propagation family. Neither involves arithmetic, natural-language
ambiguity or retrieval, and the depth axis studied here is proof depth and not
context length or problem breadth.

The condensed rendering is confounded with exposure, as described in
Section~\ref{sec:transfer-results}.

\section{Conclusion}

Holding a derivation fixed and changing only the language in which it is
written changes what a model learns from it, and the direction is set by the
depth of the derivations in the training range. Formal supervision acquires
coverage before single-sample accuracy, placing two thresholds ten depth levels
apart and making the decoding rule part of the experimental design. Because
formal derivations are checkable, that coverage converts into single-answer
accuracy without labels at a rate Proposition~\ref{prop:selector} bounds and
measurement approaches. The same dissociation appears when matched derivations
form a tenth of an ordinary midtraining mixture, against both an untreated
control and a length-matched control without deduction.
