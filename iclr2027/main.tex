\title{How Much Synthetic Logic Does an Instruction Mixture Need?}

\author{Anonymous authors}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Synthetic training data is almost always written by a language model, which
bounds it by what that model knows and passes on its errors. We study a source
with no model in it. A program samples a multi-hop deduction problem, solves
it, and writes the solution out, so every document is correct by construction.
We replace part of an ordinary instruction mixture with such derivations and
vary the share from one to twenty-five percent, holding the number of training
examples fixed at one hundred thousand and using three random seeds for every
configuration. Two effects appear at different doses. One percent of the
mixture, one thousand generated documents, takes Qwen2.5-7B from writing no
derivation that a checker accepts to writing derivations of fifty-one lines
that a checker accepts in more than nine cases out of ten, and this ability
extends to chains about twice as long as any seen in training. Accuracy on
held-out deduction benchmarks needs a larger dose and then stops improving. At
five percent, accuracy on ProofWriter at inference depth three rises from
$39.3$ to $50.0$ percent, and at ten percent of English-rendered derivations it
reaches $53.2$ percent, while accuracy on FOLIO rises from $51.4$ to $57.8$
percent. Eight general benchmarks move by less than the spread between seeds.
We then explain the gain. Instruction tuning leaves the model answering
affirmatively without regard to negation, and the synthetic derivations repair
this. The entire improvement lies in claims that are negated and false, where
accuracy rises from $17.6$ to $56.9$ percent, while the three remaining cells
of the polarity by label square do not move, and the probability that the model
answers ``true'' falls from $0.73$ to $0.50$ as the dose grows.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The public supply of human-written text is finite, and current projections
place its exhaustion for frontier training runs within this decade
\citep{villalobos2024position}. The standard remedy is synthetic text, and in
nearly every published recipe a language model writes it. Textbook-style
corpora \citep{gunasekar2023textbooks, eldan2023tinystories}, rephrased web
documents \citep{maini2024rephrasing, maini2025beyondweb, su2025nemotroncc} and
prompted expansions of a small seed corpus \citep{yang2025synthetic} all take
this form. Data of this kind inherits the generating model's errors and blind
spots, and repeated training on it narrows the distribution
\citep{shumailov2024collapse, dohmatob2025strong}. Verified synthetic data
avoids the problem in principle \citep{feng2025verification}, and individual
systems have trained on program-generated and mechanically checked derivations
at scale \citep{trinh2024alphageometry}.

A few hundred lines of Python suffice to sample a multi-hop deduction problem,
solve it, and write the solution out. The resulting documents are correct by
construction, unlimited in quantity and free of any teacher model. They are
also narrow, repetitive and unlike the web, and they describe a world of
abstract constants that carry colour and direction predicates and nothing else.
We ask how much of such material an ordinary instruction mixture needs before
the model that results reasons measurably better, and we ask what the
improvement consists of.

We answer the first question with a dose sweep. We fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B
\citep{qwen2025qwen25} on one hundred thousand instruction examples and replace
one, five, ten or twenty-five percent of them with program-generated
derivations, so that every condition sees the same number of examples and the
conditions differ only in the content of the replaced slice. We repeat every
configuration with three random seeds and we render the derivations in two
notations, a formal one and a controlled English one, drawn from the same
latent proofs.

Two effects appear, and they appear at different doses. At one percent, one
thousand documents, the model acquires the ability to write a derivation that a
checker accepts. It writes proofs of fifty-one lines at chain length
twenty-five, the checker accepts more than nine in ten of them, and the ability
extends to chains of length forty-five, about twice the longest chain in
training. A model trained on the same mixture without the replacement writes no
such derivation in six hundred attempts. At five percent the model also becomes
more accurate on held-out deduction benchmarks, and accuracy on ProofWriter
\citep{tafjord2021proofwriter} at inference depth three rises from $39.3$ to
$50.0$ percent. Ten percent of English-rendered derivations reaches $53.2$
percent and accuracy on FOLIO \citep{han2024folio} rises by $6.4$ points. The
curve is flat from five percent upward, so a quarter of the mixture buys
nothing that a twentieth does not already provide. Across eight general
benchmarks, among them GSM8K, MMLU and ARC, the arms differ by less than the
spread between seeds of the unmodified control.

We answer the second question by locating the improvement. The instruction
mixture alone leaves the model with an affirmative answer prior. It answers
that a claim holds in three cases out of four, and it answers a claim and its
negation identically in more than a third of items. The synthetic derivations
remove this. Splitting the held-out problems by the polarity of the claim and
by the gold label, the entire gain lies in one of the four cells, the claims
that are negated and false, where accuracy rises from $17.6$ to $56.9$ percent
as the dose grows. The other three cells move by a few points at most, and the
probability of answering ``true'' falls monotonically with dose from $0.73$ to
$0.50$. The improvement is therefore a correction of a decision rule that
instruction tuning installs, and we support this reading with a length-matched
control, a contamination audit, prior-invariant measures of association and
paired significance tests.

Our contributions are the following.

\begin{itemize}
\item We measure how much program-generated deduction data an instruction
      mixture needs. One percent installs the ability to write checkable
      derivations, five percent is enough for the full benchmark gain, and
      larger shares add nothing.
\item We show that the gain is a repair of an affirmative answer prior left by
      instruction tuning, and we localise it to a single cell of a polarity by
      label split of the evaluation set.
\item We show that the derivations the model writes are machine-checkable, and
      we report both where a checker converts sampling into accuracy without an
      answer key and where it fails to do so.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Synthetic data written by models.} Most synthetic pretraining
corpora are produced by prompting a language model, either to write documents
in a chosen style \citep{gunasekar2023textbooks, eldan2023tinystories} or to
rewrite existing web text \citep{maini2024rephrasing, maini2025beyondweb,
su2025nemotroncc}. The quality of the result is bounded by the generating
model, and training repeatedly on such text degrades the distribution
\citep{shumailov2024collapse, dohmatob2025strong}. Our source contains no
model, so the ceiling of the generator is the ceiling of the program that
writes it.

\paragraph{Synthetic deduction corpora.} Several corpora present deduction
problems to language models for training or evaluation, among them RuleTaker
and ProofWriter \citep{clark2020transformers, tafjord2021proofwriter}, PrOntoQA
\citep{saparov2023language} and FLD \citep{morishita2023fld, morishita2024alt}.
These corpora render their proofs in English. \citet{zhou2025finelogic} compare
symbolic and English supervision on matched problems and report that English
supervision generalises further. We generate both renderings from one latent
proof, which lets us hold the problem and the answer fixed while varying only
the notation, and we find that the ordering depends on the depth of the
training proofs.

\paragraph{Verifiable rewards and checkable traces.} Training against a
verifier has become a standard recipe for mathematics and code
\citep{lambert2025tulu, yue2025does}, and formal systems allow a proof to be
checked mechanically \citep{trinh2024alphageometry}. Our derivations are
checkable by a small forward-chaining program, which lets us ask whether a
checker can select a correct sample without an answer key, and lets us measure
how far that selection carries to a human-written benchmark.

\paragraph{Answer priors in tuned models.} Instruction-tuned models are known
to acquire response biases that are separable from their underlying competence
\citep{ouyang2022training}, and negation in particular remains difficult for
language models \citep{kassner2020negated, hosseini2021understanding,
truong2023language}. Our mechanistic result connects these two literatures. The
pathology that synthetic deduction data repairs is an affirmative answer prior
that instruction tuning installs, and the repair is visible as a change in the
prior and not as a uniform improvement in accuracy.

\section{Derivations from a Program}

The generator samples a set of constants, a set of unary predicates and a set
of implication rules, chooses a target chain length, and constructs a problem
whose answer follows from the premises by that many applications of modus
ponens. It then solves the problem by forward chaining and writes the solution
out. Nothing in the pipeline involves a language model, and every document is
correct by construction. The vocabulary is deliberately impoverished. Constants
are named $c_0$ through $c_{25}$ and the predicates are colour and direction
words, so the documents carry no world knowledge and share almost no vocabulary
with the benchmarks we later evaluate.

Each latent proof can be written in two ways. The formal rendering declares the
constants and the predicate symbols, states the premises as implications
between atoms, and lists the proof as numbered lines with the rule that
justifies each one. The English rendering states the same premises and the same
proof as ordinary sentences. The two renderings share the prompt, the answer
and the proof graph, so a comparison between them isolates notation.

We build every training mixture by replacement. A condition with a share $p$
contains $(1-p)\cdot 10^5$ examples of the instruction corpus and $p \cdot 10^5$
rendered derivations, so all conditions train on exactly one hundred thousand
examples and differ only in the content of the replaced slice. The evaluation
split of every mixture is drawn from the instruction corpus alone. This design
removes the most common confound in data-augmentation experiments, which is
that the treated condition simply sees more data.

We audit the overlap between the generated documents and the evaluation sets.
No evaluation item shares an eight-gram with any training derivation, and the
overlap of the two hundred most frequent words is four words. Words that the
benchmarks rely on, among them ``not'', ``someone'' and the animal nouns of
ProofWriter, never occur in the training derivations.

\section{How Much Synthetic Logic an Instruction Mixture Needs}

\subsection{Setup}

We fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B on each mixture for one epoch with a learning rate of
$5\times 10^{-6}$, a global batch of one hundred and twenty-eight sequences and
a context window of eight thousand one hundred and ninety-two tokens. The
window is set from measurement. The longest rendered derivation in the
twenty-five hop band occupies seven thousand seven hundred tokens, and
truncation is fail-closed, so a run aborts if more than half a percent of its
examples would be cut. We train three seeds for every configuration and report
all of them.

We evaluate on ProofWriter under the open-world assumption at inference depths
zero through five, on FOLIO, on the deduction problems of the generator at
chain lengths five through twenty-five, and on eight general benchmarks. For
the generator's own problems we prompt the model in the document format it was
trained on, since a derivation has to be requested before it can be checked.

\subsection{Results}

Table~\ref{tab:dose} reports the sweep. Two effects separate cleanly.

The first effect is the ability to write a derivation that a checker accepts,
and it is complete at one percent of the mixture. A model trained without
derivations writes none in six hundred attempts. A model trained with one
thousand of them writes a derivation in every attempt, and a forward-chaining
checker accepts more than nine in ten at chain length twenty-five. The proofs
are fifty-one lines long at that depth, which is two lines for every hop plus
the premises, and the ability extends to chains of length forty-five, about
twice the longest chain in training, where the checker still accepts nine in
ten.

The second effect is accuracy on held-out benchmarks, and it needs a larger
dose. At one percent, accuracy on ProofWriter at depth three rises by about a
point, which is within the spread between seeds. At five percent it rises from
$39.3$ to $50.0$ percent, and at ten percent of English-rendered derivations it
reaches $53.2$ percent. Every seed of every condition at five percent or more
scores above every seed of the control, and the spread among the three control
seeds is $1.4$ points. Accuracy on FOLIO rises from $51.4$ to $57.8$ percent.
Beyond five percent the curve is flat, and a condition with a quarter of the
mixture replaced scores $51.6$ percent, within noise of the five percent
condition.

The cost of the intervention is not measurable in our evaluation. Across GSM8K,
BBH, MMLU, ARC-Challenge, LogiQA, HellaSwag, PIQA and WinoGrande, no condition
differs from the control by more than the spread between control seeds.

\section{Why the Gain Occurs}

\subsection{An affirmative answer prior}

ProofWriter presents a theory and a claim and asks whether the claim is true,
false or unknown. We split the evaluation items by two properties that the
model can read off the prompt, whether the claim is negated and what the gold
label is, and we measure accuracy in each of the four resulting cells for
decidable items. Table~\ref{tab:mechanism} reports the split.

The control answers correctly on $98.5$ percent of claims that are negated and
true, on $68$ percent of claims that are positive and false, and on $75$
percent of claims that are positive and true. On claims that are negated and
false it answers correctly on $17.6$ percent. It is therefore not failing to
reason. It is answering that the claim holds, and it does so in three cases out
of four across the whole evaluation set, while the gold labels are close to
balanced.

The synthetic derivations correct this. As the dose grows, accuracy on negated
and false claims rises from $17.6$ to $56.9$ percent, the probability of
answering ``true'' falls monotonically from $0.73$ to $0.50$, and the three
remaining cells move by a few points at most. The large cell of positive and
true claims drifts down by about eight points, which is what a correction of an
affirmative prior predicts. The improvement is a change in a decision rule and
it is visible as such.

\subsection{Controls}

Three controls bound what else could produce the effect.

The first is length and domain. In a midtraining study on the same generator we
replaced the same share of the corpus with ordinary documents drawn from the
pretraining mixture and matched to the derivations in length distribution. That
condition is indistinguishable from the control on ProofWriter, with a paired
test giving $p=0.62$ and $p=1.00$ in two seeds, while the derivation condition
gains $12.6$ points. Length and domain therefore do not explain the gain.

The second is memorisation. No evaluation item shares an eight-gram with any
training derivation, and the vocabulary overlap is four words in the two
hundred most frequent.

The third is the role of deductive order. We midtrain a further condition on
the same derivations with the lines of each proof shuffled, which preserves
every token, the vocabulary, the length and the conclusion and destroys only
the order in which facts follow from one another. This condition is in training
at the time of writing and we will report it with the same measures.

\subsection{What the gain is not}

If the improvement were simply that the model reasons more, then asking the
control to reason aloud should close the gap. It does not. Under a prompt that
requests a chain of reasoning before the answer, the control reaches $39.9$
percent on ProofWriter at depth three, the five percent condition reaches $45.3$
percent and the ten percent condition reaches $49.4$ percent. A condition with
a quarter of the mixture replaced reaches $55.8$ percent, so the advantage
grows with dose in the regime where a well-prompted baseline is the natural
objection.

\section{Checkable Derivations}

A derivation written in a fixed format can be checked by a program. We
implement a forward-chaining checker that reads the premises of an item and
accepts a proof line when it is a premise or follows from lines already
established. The checker reads both notations, translating formal lines through
the predicate legend that the model itself writes. On ProofWriter it reproduces
the published gold label on $99$, $95$, $91$, $91$ and $84$ percent of items at
inference depths zero through five, and its residual errors are answers of
``unknown'' where the gold label is decided, so it under-credits.

On the generator's own problems the checker is close to an oracle. A derivation
that the checker accepts carries the correct answer in $99$ percent of samples,
and selecting the first accepted derivation out of sixteen samples gives the
same accuracy as asking whether any of the sixteen is correct. A gold-free
selection rule therefore matches sixteen-sample coverage.

On ProofWriter the same rule is much weaker. Sampling sixteen derivations per
item at temperature $0.8$, between $28$ and $37$ percent carry a proof that the
checker accepts, against none for the control, and the accuracy of an accepted
derivation is between $53$ and $69$ percent. Selection by the checker therefore
trails majority voting over the same samples. The reason is structural. The
checker certifies that the written steps follow from the premises, and
ProofWriter asks in a third of its items whether a claim is underivable, which
a forward chain never demonstrates. A checker pays where it is complete with
respect to the task and not merely sound.

\section{When Notation Matters}

The dose sweep shows the two notations transferring about equally, with the
English rendering ahead at ten percent and the formal rendering ahead at five.
A controlled study on the generator alone shows that the ordering is not fixed
and that the depth of the training proofs decides it. Fine-tuning OLMo-3-7B on
fifty thousand paired examples per condition, English supervision generalises
further when the training proofs reach at most twenty hops. At twenty-five hops
the ordering reverses, and formal supervision reaches $75.0\pm9.5$ percent
out-of-distribution accuracy against $17.8\pm4.7$ for English. Sixteen samples
move the reversal to fifteen hops, so formal supervision first widens the set
of problems the model can reach and only later improves its first attempt.

The two settings agree once the regime is stated. The formal advantage belongs
to dense and deep supervision, and an instruction mixture with a tenth of its
examples replaced is neither. We therefore do not recommend the formal
rendering on the strength of the controlled study, and we report the two
notations as interchangeable at the doses that a practitioner would use.

\section{Training Stage}

The same derivations can enter during midtraining instead. We midtrained
Qwen2.5-7B on 2.5 billion tokens with a tenth of the mixture replaced, then
applied the same instruction tuning to every condition. That route also
produces the ProofWriter gain, $12.6$ points for the English rendering at
inference depth three with two instruction-tuning seeds, and it also leaves the
general benchmarks flat, with the exception of a loss of two to three points on
LogiQA that replicates across seeds.

Comparing the two routes, midtraining is the more expensive and buys no more.
The instruction-tuning route reaches the same accuracy, generalises further on
long chains, and does not carry the LogiQA cost. Applying both in sequence adds
nothing to either, and on the generator's own problems the model trained in
both stages performs as the midtrained model alone. What the midtrained model
retains is an ability that instruction tuning without derivations removes
completely. Prompted in the format of its training documents, the midtrained
model writes checkable proofs to chain length twenty-five, and after ordinary
instruction tuning it writes none.

\section{Limitations}

Our gains are confined to deduction. ProofWriter and FOLIO improve, the eight
general benchmarks do not move, and we have no evidence that the intervention
helps tasks that do not ask for a chain of inferences.

The mechanism we identify is a correction of an answer prior. This is a real
and replicated effect, and it is a narrower claim than an improvement in
reasoning capability. A model whose prior is already balanced may gain less.

Our checker is sound and incomplete. It certifies that written steps follow and
it cannot certify that a fact is underivable, which limits the benefit of
checker-based selection on benchmarks with an open-world answer.

We train one model family at one scale. The affirmative prior that the
derivations repair is a property of the instruction corpus we use, and a
different instruction corpus may leave a different pathology.

\section{Conclusion}

A program that writes deduction problems and their solutions produces training
data with no teacher model in it, and a small amount of that data changes what
an instruction-tuned language model does. One percent of an ordinary mixture is
enough for the model to write proofs that a checker accepts, at chain lengths
about twice those it saw in training, where a model trained without the
replacement writes none. Five percent is enough for the full improvement on
held-out deduction benchmarks, and larger shares add nothing. The improvement
is a correction of an affirmative answer prior that instruction tuning installs,
it is confined to the claims where that prior does damage, and it costs nothing
on the general benchmarks we measure.
